# AI contract clauses checklist

Have qualified counsel adapt these issues to the transaction and jurisdictions; this is an operational checklist, not legal advice.

- [ ] Defined AI system/service, models, features, agents, tools, data flows, subprocessors, intended uses, and prohibited uses.
- [ ] Customer data, prompts, outputs, telemetry, feedback, and derived data may not be used for training except as explicitly agreed.
- [ ] Data ownership/licence, confidentiality, retention, deletion, location, transfer, backup, and return are explicit.
- [ ] Security controls cover SDLC, access, encryption, tenant isolation, vulnerabilities, testing, dependencies, and personnel.
- [ ] Agent permissions, tool connections, delegated credentials, memory, transaction/spend limits, and human-approval boundaries are explicit.
- [ ] Supplier provides current system/model documentation, evaluation evidence, limitations, and customer configuration guidance.
- [ ] Performance, safety, fairness, accessibility, availability, recovery, and support commitments are measurable where material.
- [ ] Supplier gives advance notice of material model, feature, tool, policy, subprocessor, location, and terms changes.
- [ ] Customer can pin, test, defer, or roll back material versions where operationally necessary.
- [ ] Incident definition includes harmful output/action, privacy/security breach, systemic performance failure, and legal noncompliance.
- [ ] Notification deadlines, content, evidence preservation, cooperation, remediation, root-cause analysis, and regulator support are defined.
- [ ] Customer has usable logs, trace export, monitoring integration, audit rights, and evidence access proportionate to risk.
- [ ] Supplier supports impact assessments, individual rights, explanations, complaints, contestability, and records obligations.
- [ ] Responsibility, indemnity, liability, insurance, IP/input/output claims, and regulatory change are allocated consciously.
- [ ] Suspension, kill switch, termination, deletion verification, export/portability, transition, and business continuity are workable.
- [ ] Flow-down obligations cover model providers, hosting, data providers, tool vendors, and other material subprocessors.
- [ ] Assurances survive termination where needed; conflicts between online policies and negotiated terms are resolved.
