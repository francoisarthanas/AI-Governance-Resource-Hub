## 1. Appropriateness and scope

- What legitimate objective and measurable benefit is sought?
- Is AI necessary and proportionate? What non-AI alternatives were evaluated?
- What is the system boundary, lifecycle, operating context, scale, and duration?
- Who uses, develops, supplies, operates, reviews, and is affected by it?
- What intended, reasonably foreseeable, unintended, and prohibited uses exist?
- What assumptions and unknowns could change this assessment?

## 2. Affected parties and impact pathways

Identify directly and indirectly affected people, groups, workers, customers, non-users, communities, and environments. Include people with disabilities, children, vulnerable or historically disadvantaged groups where relevant.

For each impact pathway, describe:

- source/cause and system behavior;
- benefit and who receives it;
- potential harm and who bears it;
- severity (scale, scope, duration, reversibility) and likelihood/exposure;
- cumulative, downstream, distributional, societal, and environmental effects;
- evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, and affected-party input.

## 4. Data and privacy

- Necessity, proportionality, purpose limitation, minimization, and lawful authority/basis:
- Data provenance, rights, consent, representativeness, quality, labeling, and sensitive inference:
- Training, fine-tuning, retrieval, prompting, telemetry, feedback, reuse, sharing, and provider use:
- Access, security, residency, retention, deletion, de-identification, re-identification, and individual rights:

Link the formal privacy/DPIA record when triggered.

## 5. Safety, security, and abuse

- Safety-critical outcomes and foreseeable misuse/dual use:
- Threat actors, assets, trust boundaries, attack paths, and supply-chain risks:
- Manipulation, prompt injection, model/data theft, poisoning, evasion, leakage, insecure output, and availability:
- Physical, psychological, financial, legal, operational, democratic, environmental, and reputational harm:
- Safe failure, fallback, continuity, containment, rollback, recovery, and vulnerability/incident disclosure:

Link the threat model and security/safety assessments.

## 6. Human oversight and accountability

- Who is responsible and accountable at every material decision/action?
- What does the human see, know, decide, approve, override, and record?
- Are reviewers trained, independent enough, timely, resourced, and empowered?
- How will automation bias, alert fatigue, approval volume, disagreement, and override quality be measured?
- Who can pause, revoke, correct, compensate, notify, and authorize resumption?

## 7. Agentic AI addendum

If applicable, link the agent assessment and summarize:

- authority exercised and maximum autonomy by action;
- identities, credentials, tools, data, memory, networks, and delegation;
- action reversibility and human gates;
- independent limits, policy enforcement, validation, isolation, and observability;
- cascading, multi-agent, emergent, runaway, and accountability risks;
- tested containment and recovery.
