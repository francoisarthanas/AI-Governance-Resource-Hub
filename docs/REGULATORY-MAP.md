# Regulatory Map: Official Entry Points

AI obligations rarely live in one “AI law.” Existing privacy, consumer, employment, anti-discrimination, product safety, cybersecurity, intellectual property, records, accessibility, financial, medical, and sector rules continue to apply.

This page is a routing aid, not legal advice or a compliance determination.

## The five questions to answer

1. **Where?** Where are provider, deployer, users, affected people, data, models, and actions located?
2. **Who?** Are you provider/developer, deployer/user, importer, distributor, product manufacturer, employer, controller/processor, regulated entity, or public authority?
3. **What?** Is this an AI system, GPAI model, automated decision, profiling, biometric system, safety component, medical device, consumer product, employment tool, or agent?
4. **Impact?** Is the use prohibited, high/consequential risk, transparency-limited risk, safety critical, or otherwise regulated?
5. **When?** Which effective date, transition, guidance, standard, code, enforcement authority, and reporting window applies?

Record the analysis owner, source, date checked, conclusion, assumptions, obligations, evidence, and recheck trigger.

- [Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI](https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence) — verify signatures, ratifications, entry into force, reservations, and implementing law for relevant states.

## United States

There is no single general federal AI statute that replaces existing law. Track federal, state, local, and sector obligations.

- [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf) — covered federal agency AI use and governance.
- [OMB M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf) — covered federal AI acquisition.
- [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — voluntary risk framework, often referenced in policy and contracts.
- [FTC guidance and cases on AI](https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence) — consumer protection and competition enforcement entry point.
- [EEOC guidance on AI and the ADA](https://www.eeoc.gov/eeoc-disability-related-resources/artificial-intelligence-and-ada) — employment discrimination and disability resources for AI-based assessment.
- [Federal Reserve SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm) — current interagency model-risk guidance for covered banking organizations.
- [NAIC AI work](https://content.naic.org/committees/h/big-data-artificial-intelligence-wg) — insurance model bulletin and state adoption work.
- [FDA digital health guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/guidances-digital-health-content) — current medical-device guidance.

Use official state legislatures, regulators, attorneys general, and city sources for state/local requirements. Verify effective dates, amendments, court decisions, preemption, scope, and regulator rules at the time of the decision.

## Keep the map current

- Assign a legal/regulatory owner and named local counsel where needed.
- Subscribe to regulator and standards-body updates, not only newsletters about them.
- Record “last checked” and the specific version/effective date in every assessment.
- Recheck on new jurisdiction, use, role, affected group, provider, model capability, incident, or material guidance/law change.
- Preserve the source and reasoning that supported each decision.

Never represent an automated compliance checker, crosswalk, certification, or vendor platform as legal advice.
